Experience

A timeline of roles in GIS, conservation, and applied spatial research.

Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation ('25 - present)

Conservation Data Specialist

In this role, I support Virginia's dam safety and flood risk programs by building and maintaining the GIS infrastructure behind them, including the Virginia Flood Risk Information System (VFRIS) and Dam Safety Inventory System (DSIS). Day to day, that means a mix of routine data upkeep and more involved spatial analysis and application development for agency planning and reporting. I lean heavily on Python to keep workflows reproducible and cut down on manual, repetitive tasks, and I write SQL queries to pull, QA, and structure data from relational databases. I also spend a fair amount of time on the documentation side — writing metadata, user guides, and workflow notes so that the tools and data I build are actually usable by the colleagues who inherit them.

NOAA NCCOS, Coastal Resilience Branch ('23 - '25)

Product Development Specialist

Here, I translated coastal science into products people could actually use — story maps, dashboards, and infographics aimed at helping coastal managers and stakeholders make sense of sea-level rise and resilience risk. Most of this work lived in the ArcGIS Online ecosystem (Experience Builder, Dashboards) alongside ArcGIS Pro, Python, and Adobe Illustrator for the more design-forward pieces like one-pagers. A big part of the job was translation in the literal sense — taking inundation modeling and turning it into something a non-scientist could understand at a glance — and I made sure everything I shipped, from web apps to static graphics, met Section 508 accessibility requirements. QA/QC was baked into the process throughout rather than a final step.

St. Croix, USVI & Monterey, California ('20 - '24)

Graduate Researcher — Spatial Ecology of Leatherback Sea Turtles

My thesis research centered on tracking leatherback sea turtles to better understand how they use habitat during the breeding season. Working with federal and nonprofit partners, I helped deploy satellite tags on nesting females on St. Croix and followed their movements well beyond the nesting season. Analyzing that tracking data in R and ArcGIS Pro let me map out key in-water habitat areas, classify different behavioral states, and tie movement patterns to specific environmental variables.

Upwell Turtles, Monterey, California ('21 - '22)

Observer

I flew aerial line-transect surveys off the coast of Monterey, spotting and logging sea turtles, marine mammals, and jellyfish. The data fed into broader assessments of species distribution and density — for turtles, cetaceans, and their prey alike — along with the oceanographic conditions shaping their habitat.

University Corporation, Monterey, California ('21 - '22)

GIS Analyst

For a project with the California Public Utilities Commission, I applied geostatistical methods — semivariogram modeling and kriging — in ArcGIS Pro to build raster surfaces mapping cell coverage by carrier. Beyond the analysis itself, I put together training materials and QA/QC documentation and walked the client through them directly, and I built a custom geoprocessing toolbox to speed up the workflow going forward.

Timmons Group, Richmond, Virginia ('19 - '21)

GIS Specialist / GIS Technician

Across these two roles, I kept client GIS databases current and accurate, turning raw field and survey data into usable spatial datasets through digitizing, coordinate geometry, and manual and automated data entry. I worked directly with clients on environmental resource management projects, which meant translating their needs into deliverables our team could execute on cleanly. I held myself to quality standards above what clients required, running both manual reviews and Data Reviewer checks, and regularly put together training materials and presentations to hand off project knowledge.

University of Richmond, School of Professional and Continuing Studies ('19 - '21)

St. Croix Sea Turtle Project / USFWS / NOAA, Sandy Point National Wildlife Refuge ('17, '18, '19)

Leatherback Sea Turtle Research Assistant

I handled the geospatial side of the project's data too, using Access, Excel, and ArcGIS to manage and QA both historic and current records, and regularly presented findings and updates to project partners and the public.

Department of Geography and the Environment, University of Richmond ('18 - '19)

Research Assistant & Part-Time GIS Technician

I split my time between mentoring a group of eight undergraduate and high school interns — overseeing their work, resolving the inevitable conflicts, and keeping morale up — and contributing directly to faculty and student research. Alongside the fieldwork, I analyzed restoration outcomes using GIS and built web applications to help the organization communicate its impact.
